# Review: TIC Research Memo

**Verdict: YELLOW** — substantively sound but contains one regulation misquote, an outdated UPHPA enactment count, an SBA characterization that is incomplete, an overstated case description, and a master-lease cure recommendation that contains a non-trivial overstatement. Most citations are accurate. The user-facing structural advice is legally usable but missing important caveats.

## Verified accurate

- Treas. Reg. §301.7701-1(a)(2) substance (mere co-ownership not a separate entity; services to occupants can create one)
- Rev. Proc. 2002-22 Section 6 conditions (§§6.01–6.15) — substantive content matches published text
- Rev. Rul. 75-374 holding (two-person apartment-building TIC + agent for customary services not a partnership)
- Madison Gas & Electric Co. v. Commissioner, 633 F.2d 512 (7th Cir. 1980) — citation and partnership-recharacterization holding correct
- United States v. Craft, 535 U.S. 274 (2002) — citation correct
- Treas. Reg. §301.7701-3 check-the-box mechanics
- §1031(a)(2) exclusion of partnership interests
- Howey test elements
- Single-loan/multiple-borrower SBA practice
- Master-lease rent rule under §6.13 (fixed gross-receipts % allowed; net income/profits not)

## Errors found

### 1. Misquote of Treas. Reg. §301.7701-1(a)(2) — sequence inversion
- Memo's quote inverts the actual order of sentences in the regulation. The actual regulation places the "Mere co-ownership" sentence **before** the joint-venture explanation, includes a "farm property lease" example sentence the memo omits, and follows with the expense-sharing sentence.
- Severity: Moderate. Substance is right; quotation marks misrepresent text. **Re-write as paraphrase or restore exact paragraph order.**

### 2. UPHPA enactment count outdated
- Memo: "over 20 states"
- Actual: 24 states + D.C. + U.S. Virgin Islands (Michigan and New Jersey enacted in 2025)
- Severity: Low

### 3. Bergford v. Commissioner — overstated factual recital
- Memo claim "78 investors" doesn't appear cleanly in Ninth Circuit opinion
- Memo's quoted phrase "limitations on the co-owners' ability to sell, lease, or encumber" appears to be a paraphrase, not verbatim from the published opinion
- Severity: Moderate — putting unverified phrase in quotation marks is the issue

### 4. SBA — TIC characterization incomplete
- Memo doesn't surface the most important SBA-specific point: under SOP 50 10 8, **a TIC arrangement holding the real property is treated by SBA as ONE EPC**, not multiple EPCs. Multiple EPCs are not allowed.
- This is a planning-level fact the user needs.

### 5. SOP version outdated in source list
- Memo: "SBA SOP 50 10" generic
- Actual: SOP 50 10 8, effective June 1, 2025

## Overstatements / hedges needed

### A. "Safe harbor" framing
- Memo headers "15-Factor Safe Harbor" suggests binary compliance.
- Actual: Rev. Proc. 2002-22 is a **ruling-request procedure**. Failure to satisfy §6 does not automatically convert TIC to partnership; satisfaction does not bind IRS at audit. The §6 factors remain the most authoritative IRS roadmap, but framing should be "ruling conditions commonly called a safe harbor."

### B. Master-lease cure has real complications
- Memo presents JV LLC as master tenant as clean fix. Actual issues:
  1. JV LLC sitting on both sides (cotenant + lessee) creates §6.13 "bona fide lease" tension and parallels §6.12's prohibition on manager-as-lessee.
  2. Mandatory long-term master lease can itself be a securities-law indicator (memo's own §8 lists this as Howey-investment-contract trigger).
  3. Percentage-of-gross-receipts must be FIXED %; structuring to mirror JV waterfall (net cash flow dependent) defeats the purpose.
- Add caveat paragraph; consider an unaffiliated operating company as master tenant.

### C. "Same economic outcome — no more, no less" is structurally unachievable (HIGH PRIORITY)
- Memo proposes setting Janet/Ryan undivided percentages so pro-rata cash flow = JV waterfall result.
- Reality: A pro-rata percentage matches a JV waterfall only at ONE expected return scenario. Above the waterfall preference, JV partners get the promote that TICs can't mirror. Below, TIC losses fall pro-rata in a way they wouldn't under JV protective structures.
- **The "no more, no less" standard is unachievable inside a safe-harbor TIC.**

## Bottom line for synthesis (5 bullets)

1. The memo is fundamentally correct and well-organized but contains one regulation-misquote, one outdated count, and one unverified inline quote that should be paraphrased.
2. The "safe harbor" framing is rhetorically imprecise; failure to satisfy §6 is not automatic disqualification. Fix the framing.
3. **The memo does not adequately answer the user's central question.** A static-percentage TIC cannot replicate a curved JV waterfall across return scenarios; "same economic outcome — no more, no less" is structurally unachievable inside a safe-harbor TIC. The master-lease cure has real §6.13/Howey tension that the memo does not reconcile.
4. SBA-specific point missing: under SOP 50 10 8, the three-cotenant arrangement is treated as a single EPC, and multiple EPCs are not allowed.
5. Significant tax-mechanics gaps (depreciation, §469, §163(j), §199A, separate-exit 1031s, *Craft*-style lien attachment, community-property overlays, partition risk) — should be added before driving any closing-document drafting.
