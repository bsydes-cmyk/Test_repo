# Review: Precedents Research Memo

**Verdict: GREEN-leaning-YELLOW**

The memo's citations are overwhelmingly real, the legal holdings are accurately summarized, and there are **no fabricated cases or PLRs**. However, there are a few non-trivial mis-descriptions (one possibly mis-linked Bluerock exhibit; one slightly over-stated SBA SOP claim), and the memo's "closest match" framing oversells the relevance of large-cap REIT precedents to a $1.9M hospitality deal.

## Errors found

1. **Robinson v. Glynn — board seat description.** Memo: "two of the three board seats." Actual: GeoPhone board had **seven** members; Robinson appointed two and held one seat himself. Fix to "two of seven board seats."
2. **Bluerock Ex. 10.3 attribution** — see above. Either fix to correct property ("Fox Hills") or remove.
3. **SBA SOP 50 10 8 — quoted-text claim.** Memo says "Authoritative text confirms..." Secondary sources support SBA *policy* but exact quoted SOP language not independently verified. Pin claim to specific SOP section number with verbatim quotation, or soften to "SBA practitioner guidance under SOP 50 10 confirms."
4. **CityCenter Boutique Hotel OA — utility caveat missing.** Exhibit is real but is a single-member, manager-managed OA (CityCenter Land, LLC as sole member) for a disregarded entity inside the MGM/Dubai World CityCenter mega-project. Not a multi-member JV OA. Memo over-sells it as a "boutique-hotel JV LLC operating agreement."
5. **Bluerock 23Hundred deal structure framing** — essentially correct, but BGF was not a "joining" 1031 investor — the redemption was driven by REIT-level UPREIT/tax-planning logic for an institutional sponsor in advance of a sale, not an outside 1031 investor coming in.

## Gaps the memo missed

1. **DST cases / Rev. Rul. 2004-86** — Delaware Statutory Trust ruling, the natural pair to Rev. Proc. 2002-22 for 1031 sidecars. Many modern 1031 fundraises use DSTs rather than TICs because of the "seven deadly sins" limits on TICs.
2. **Rev. Proc. 2009-3 / post-2009 PLR moratorium** — IRS *stopped* issuing affirmative PLRs under Rev. Proc. 2002-22 in 2009. User cannot get a comfort PLR today. Material context point.
3. **Howey itself (SEC v. W.J. Howey Co., 328 U.S. 293 (1946))** — foundational case referenced but not formally cited.
4. **SBA OHA Office of Hearings & Appeals decisions** — analogous size/affiliation cases relevant when 6-8 members each hold less than 20% but collectively run an OC.
5. **Smaller hotel JV cases** — *Long v. Schultz Cattle Co.*, 881 F.2d 129 (5th Cir. 1989) (cattle JV held to be security on Williamson) for small-deal/active-investor analogue.
6. **Hocking v. Dubois follow-up** — *Salameh v. Tarsadia Hotel* (9th Cir. 2013), modern condo-hotel-rental-pool case applying Hocking. Directly on-point for STR/boutique-hotel structuring.
7. **SEC No-Action Letters on real estate JVs** — *Woodbridge Mortgage Investment Fund* and similar.

## Practical Utility Assessment

**Mixed and overstated.**

- User's deal: $1.9M, 6-8 active members, small TIC sidecar, hospitality/STR.
- Bluerock Stonehenge 23Hundred: **$61.2M institutional Class A multifamily REIT JV** with institutional sponsor and regional developer. Structural form (TIC bolted onto JV LLC) portable; commercial dynamics, governance, capital scale, investor sophistication NOT.
- A user-side lawyer drafting from Bluerock's exhibits will produce a document drastically over-engineered for a $1.9M deal with seven friends.
- **PLRs 200513010 and 200327003 are the most directly portable items** — useful as decision-rights templates and on the manager-discretion question.
- **Williamson/Robinson/Hocking line is fully on-point and properly cited.**
- **CityCenter Boutique Hotel OA is essentially window-dressing** — single-member disregarded entity inside a mega-project. Memo's claim that user "can study its waterfall, manager-removal, and fee provisions" overstates what's in the exhibit.
- **Hospitality Investors Trust bankruptcy docket** is "goldmine" only if user has time to mine it; not a precedent in legal sense, just a deposit of forms.
- **SBA SOP 50 10 8** correctly identified as operative regulation but memo doesn't grapple with practical difficulty: with 6-8 members, **passive equity members in TIC sidecar may be deemed affiliates of the OC under SBA size/control rules, blowing the EPC structure.**

**Bottom line on utility:** Memo provides credible authority backbone but oversells how directly the precedents apply. Treat Bluerock and CityCenter as **form precedents** (sources of clauses), the PLRs and Williamson/Robinson/Hocking as **legal precedents** (sources of rules), and recognize that no precedent in the memo squarely matches a $1.9M small-active-JV-plus-1031-sidecar in hospitality.

## Bottom line for synthesis (5 bullets)

1. Citations are real and not fabricated; legal holdings are accurately summarized; this is a credible memo, not a hallucinated one.
2. Three non-trivial fixes needed: (i) verify or relabel Bluerock Ex. 10.3 attribution, (ii) correct Robinson v. Glynn board-seat count to "two of seven," (iii) tone down SBA SOP and CityCenter claims.
3. Memo's "closest match: Bluerock 23Hundred" framing is *structurally* defensible but *commercially* overstated — a $61M institutional REIT JV is not a clean analogue for a $1.9M small-active-JV hospitality deal.
4. Real precedent gaps: DST authority (Rev. Rul. 2004-86), post-2009 PLR moratorium (Rev. Proc. 2009-3), *Salameh v. Tarsadia Hotel*, *Long v. Schultz Cattle*, and SBA affiliation/size risk for TIC sidecar investors.
5. Most directly usable precedents for user's deal: PLRs 200327003 and 200513010 (decision-rights and manager-discretion templates) plus the Williamson/Robinson/Hocking trio (doctrinal frame). Bluerock and CityCenter exhibits useful as drafting forms only, with explicit acknowledgment of scale mismatch.
