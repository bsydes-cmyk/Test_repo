# Review: 1031 Research Memo

**Verdict: YELLOW — use with corrections.**

The memo is structurally sound, the §6 condition-by-condition framework is largely accurate, and the practical guidance is mostly correct. However, there are factual errors in case citations (particularly *Chase*), one citation error on the T.D. 9935 effective date, an overstatement about §1031(a)(2), an inaccuracy regarding email as an authorized identification method, a partly-incorrect summary of Rev. Rul. 79-77, and several gaps that materially affect the user's deal.

## Errors found

### 1. **Chase v. Commissioner — WRONG citation** (HIGH PRIORITY)
- Memo claim: "*Chase v. Commissioner*, T.C. Memo. 2004-142"
- Actual: **Chase v. Commissioner, 92 T.C. 874 (1989)** — regular Tax Court opinion, not a Memo, from 1989. Concerned John Muir Investors LP, applied substance-over-form to recharacterize partnership-level sale.
- Source: scotusreports.com summary

### 2. T.D. 9935 effective date
- Memo: "Nov. 23, 2020"
- Actual: filed Nov 23, **published in Federal Register and effective December 2, 2020**.
- Source: 85 FR 77365

### 3. Rev. Rul. 79-77 mischaracterized
- Memo: "Three individual co-owners of a commercial building subject to a long-term net lease"
- Actual: Rev. Rul. 79-77 concerned **a trust** (A, B, C transferred title to a trust with bank as trustee, naming themselves beneficiaries). The IRS held it was a trust, not a business entity. Memo's TIC framing is inaccurate.

### 4. Email as authorized identification — overstated
- Memo: "...emailed before midnight day 45"
- Actual: §1.1031(k)-1(c)(2) authorizes "hand delivered, mailed, telecopied, or **otherwise sent**." Most QIs accept email under "otherwise sent" but it's not enumerated.

### 5. §1031(a)(2) statutory description — outdated
- Memo cites "§1031(a)(2)(D)" — this **does not exist post-TCJA**. The pre-TCJA enumerated list (stocks, partnership interests, etc.) was deleted. Post-TCJA, §1031(a)(2) reads simply: "This subsection shall not apply to any exchange of real property held primarily for sale."
- Fix: cite §1031(a)(1) and Reg. §1.1031(a)-3 instead.

### 6. §6.04 vs §6.06 conflation
- Memo places "right of first offer at FMV before partition" in §6.04. Correct location is §6.06 (Restrictions on Alienation).

## Overstatements / hedges needed

1. **Exec Summary "JV-LLC + TIC structure can work"** — overstated; reword to "is **defensible** if rigorously documented." No PLR has ruled on a directly-analogous fact pattern.
2. **§1 drafting caution** — add explicit statement that even full §6 satisfaction does not guarantee co-ownership treatment if *Luna* eight-factor analysis cuts the other way.
3. **§7C "Master Lease cleanly insulates the TIC pool"** — overstated. Reword to "substantially mitigates the partnership-recharacterization risk."
4. **§10 "24+ months holding period"** — heuristic, not a defensive rule. *Reesink* upheld ~7 months; *Adams* denied a longer hold. Intent and use facts ultimately control.

## Gaps that matter for the deal (prioritized)

1. **(HIGH) §721 trap on exit** — *Magneson* permits §1031 + §721, but the IRS can apply the same logic adversely on the back end. If Janet/Ryan later contribute their TIC to the JV LLC, the IRS can recharacterize. Add exit-path planning.
2. **(HIGH) §1250 depreciation recapture** — never mentioned. Recapture can be triggered to extent of boot recognized.
3. **(HIGH) Basis allocation among TICs** — Janet/Ryan's bases (acquired via §1031) will diverge sharply from JV LLC's basis. Affects depreciation schedules and Schedule E.
4. **(MEDIUM-HIGH) Boot from debt-share mismatch** — if Janet's relinquished debt > her pro-rata share of new $575K SBA, she has net debt relief = boot. Deal-killer to model before identification.
5. **(MEDIUM) State law — partition rights vs. lender restrictions** — most commercial lenders insist on partition waivers; tension with §6.06.
6. **(MEDIUM) SBA 7(a) eligibility** — passive real estate holders generally ineligible; need EPC/OC structure.
7. **(LOW-MEDIUM) State income-tax conformity** — California decoupled rules; FTB Form 3840 clawback.
8. **(MEDIUM) Master lease related-party rent issue** — JV LLC as both cotenant and lessee creates §6.13 self-dealing tension.
9. **(LOW) Sponsor-affiliated manager fees** — recommend fairness opinion analogous to acquisition fee.
10. **(LOW-MEDIUM) Independent tax opinion allocation** in JV docs.

## Bottom line for synthesis (5 bullets)

1. The §6 condition-by-condition analysis is reliable except §6.04/§6.06 conflation and the corrected Rev. Rul. 79-77 framing.
2. Replace the *Chase* citation with **Chase v. Commissioner, 92 T.C. 874 (1989)** — black-letter error.
3. The structure is defensible, not safe. Add a tax-opinion line item and explicit *Luna* eight-factor sanity check at closing.
4. Four risk areas should be added before this memo informs deal documents: §721 exit trap, §1250 recapture, basis divergence among TICs, debt-share-mismatch boot.
5. The "JV LLC must not be both lessee and manager" rule is correct and load-bearing. The user should confirm in the term sheet that the JV LLC plays exactly one role.
