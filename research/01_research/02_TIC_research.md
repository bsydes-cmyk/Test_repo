# MEMO: Tenancy-in-Common (TIC) Co-Ownership of Real Estate

**Prepared for:** Janet Ng / Ryan TIC interest in $1.9M acquisition (alongside JV LLC; $575K SBA loan; 1031 funds)
**Sources:** Primary government materials only — IRS Revenue Procedures and Rulings, Treasury Regulations, federal court opinions, SEC, and SBA SOPs/forms.
**Date:** May 8, 2026

---

## Executive Summary

- **TIC is a creature of state property law.** Each tenant in common owns a separate, undivided fractional fee interest, freely alienable and devisable, with the right to seek partition. There is no federal "TIC statute"; the parties' rights are defined by the law of the state where the real property sits.
- **For federal income tax, mere co-ownership is not a partnership.** Treas. Reg. §301.7701-1(a)(2) draws the line: "mere co-ownership of property that is maintained, kept in repair, and rented or leased does not constitute a separate entity for federal tax purposes," but a joint venture or contractual arrangement where the parties "carry on a trade, business, financial operation, or venture and divide the profits therefrom" does.
- **Rev. Proc. 2002-22 sets the IRS safe harbor** for ruling that an undivided fractional interest in rental real property is not an interest in a business entity. It contains 15 conditions in Section 6 (cap of 35 co-owners, unanimity on major decisions, no entity-level holding, proportionate sharing of revenue and debt, FMV management/lease/sponsor fees, no business activities beyond customary maintenance and repair, etc.). Failure to satisfy a condition is **not** automatic disqualification — it just means no ruling will be issued. The factors are still the IRS's roadmap of what pushes co-ownership toward partnership.
- **§1031 like-kind exchange treatment of TIC interests** is the entire reason Janet Ng and Ryan are taking title as TICs rather than as members of the JV LLC. An undivided fractional interest in real property is real property for §1031 purposes; an interest in a partnership/LLC is not (IRC §1031(a)(2); Rev. Proc. 2002-22 §3.02). If the TIC arrangement is recharacterized as a partnership, their inbound 1031 funds fail.
- **Securities and lender risks must be managed.** A TIC offered with a packaged management/lease arrangement and pooled economics can become an "investment contract" under SEC v. W.J. Howey Co., 328 U.S. 293 (1946). On the lending side, federally regulated lenders and the SBA generally require all title-holders to sign as co-borrowers and grant a single first-priority lien on the entire fee — a single note with multiple borrowers, jointly and severally liable.

## 1. State-Law Foundation of TIC

Tenancy in common is the default form of concurrent ownership at common law. Each cotenant holds a separate, undivided fractional fee interest, with four distinguishing attributes:

1. **Unity of possession only.** Each tenant has the right to possess the entire property, not just her percentage; the interests are "undivided."
2. **Freely alienable.** A cotenant may sell, devise, mortgage, or lease her own interest without the consent of the others (subject to private contractual restraints).
3. **No survivorship.** Unlike joint tenancy, when a TIC dies, the interest passes by will or intestacy.
4. **Right to partition.** Any cotenant may force a partition in kind or by sale through a state-court partition action.

**Uniform Partition of Heirs Property Act (UPHPA).** Adopted in over 20 states, modifies the default partition remedy when "heirs property" is involved.

**Uniform Real Property Transfer on Death Act (URPTODA).** Adopted in roughly half the states.

**State law confirmation.** Counsel for Janet and Ryan must confirm in the property's state: (i) any statutory restraint on private agreements limiting partition rights and alienation; (ii) recording requirements for the TIC agreement and the fractional deed; (iii) homestead and community-property overlays where relevant; (iv) whether a UPHPA-style buyout regime would be triggered.

## 2. IRS Treatment of TIC

### 2.1 The regulatory line — Treas. Reg. §301.7701-1(a)(2)

> "A joint venture or other contractual arrangement may create a separate entity for federal tax purposes if the participants carry on a trade, business, financial operation, or venture and divide the profits therefrom. For example, a separate entity exists for federal tax purposes if co-owners of an apartment building lease space and in addition provide services to the occupants either directly or through an agent. Nevertheless, a joint undertaking merely to share expenses does not create a separate entity for federal tax purposes. **Mere co-ownership of property that is maintained, kept in repair, and rented or leased does not constitute a separate entity for federal tax purposes.**" — 26 CFR §301.7701-1(a)(2).

### 2.2 Check-the-box — Treas. Reg. §301.7701-3
An "eligible entity" with two or more members defaults to a partnership unless it elects on Form 8832 to be classified as a corporation. The threshold question — "is there an entity at all?" — is answered under §301.7701-1.

### 2.3 Rev. Rul. 75-374 — TIC + management agent ≠ partnership
Two-person co-ownership of an apartment building. Cotenants employed an agent to collect rents, pay taxes, insurance and maintenance, and provide tenants with "customary services." Service held the arrangement was not a partnership.

### 2.4 Rev. Rul. 79-77
Confirmed that a co-ownership of farmland between two owners, with each individually responsible for his proportionate share of expenses and revenues, was not a partnership.

### 2.5 §1031 application
§1031(a)(1) defers gain on the exchange of real property held for productive use in a trade or business or for investment. §1031(a)(2) excludes "interests in a partnership." Treasury and IRS have long accepted that an undivided fractional interest in real property is itself real property for §1031.

### 2.6 Federal cases recharacterizing co-ownership as partnership
- **Bergford v. Commissioner, 12 F.3d 166 (9th Cir. 1993).** 78 investors purchased "co-ownership" interests in computer equipment subject to a 7-year net lease. Held: a partnership for federal tax purposes — "limitations on the co-owners' ability to sell, lease, or encumber either the co-ownership interest or the underlying property and the manager's effective participation in both profits and losses" defeated TIC characterization.
- **Bussing v. Commissioner, 88 T.C. 449 (1987).**
- **Madison Gas & Electric Co. v. Commissioner, 633 F.2d 512 (7th Cir. 1980).**
- **Allison v. Commissioner, T.C. Memo 1976-248.**

## 3. TIC vs. Partnership — Federal Tax Line (Comparison Table)

| Fact pattern | Pushes toward TIC (no entity) | Pushes toward Partnership |
|---|---|---|
| Title | Each cotenant on the deed for an undivided fractional fee | Title held by an entity, or deed silent on percentages |
| Number of cotenants | Small, identifiable group; safe harbor caps at 35 | Many investors, syndicated |
| Tax filing | Each cotenant reports proportionate share on Schedule E; no Form 1065 | Single Form 1065 with K-1s; aggregated bookkeeping |
| EIN/bank accounts | No common EIN; rents flow through to cotenants pro rata | Common EIN, common operating account, joint books |
| Holding-out | Property leased "by the cotenants"; no business name | "Doing business as" or trade name; common letterhead |
| Major decisions | Unanimous consent for sale, refi, hiring manager, leasing | Majority vote; manager has discretion |
| Restraints on alienation | None or only narrow ROFR/ROFO at FMV; partition right preserved | Lock-up, drag-along, mandatory consent of all members |
| Profit/loss sharing | Strictly proportionate to undivided interest | Special allocations, preferred return, promote/carry |
| Debt | Each owner liable on her share, or proportionate guaranty | Recourse pooled at entity level only |
| Services to tenants | Customary landlord services only (per Rev. Rul. 75-374) | Hotel-type, concierge, services tied to occupancy |
| Management | Renewable annually, FMV fee, not contingent on net profits | Long-term, contingent on cash flow / net profits / promote |
| Sponsor / promote | None, or one-time FMV acquisition fee | Carried interest from net proceeds beyond a typical fee |

## 4. Rev. Proc. 2002-22 — The 15-Factor Safe Harbor (Quoted)

Source: Rev. Proc. 2002-22, 2002-1 C.B. 733, https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-02-22.pdf.

**Threshold scope (§3.02):** "This revenue procedure provides guidelines for [requesting] a ruling that an undivided fractional interest in rental real property … is not an interest in a business entity, within the meaning of §301.7701-2(a)."

**§6.01 Tenancy in Common Ownership.** "Each of the co-owners must hold title to the Property … as a tenant in common under local law. Thus, title to the Property as a whole may not be held by an entity recognized under local law."

**§6.02 Number of Co-Owners.** "The number of co-owners must be limited to no more than 35 persons. For this purpose, 'person' is defined as in §7701(a)(1) … and a husband and wife are treated as a single person, and all persons who acquire interests from a co-owner by inheritance are treated as a single person."

**§6.03 No Treatment of Co-Ownership as an Entity.** "The co-ownership may not file a partnership or corporate tax return, conduct business under a common name, execute an agreement identifying any or all of the co-owners as partners, shareholders, or members of a business entity, or otherwise hold itself out as a partnership or other form of business entity (nor may the co-owners hold themselves out as partners, shareholders, or members of a business entity)."

**§6.04 Co-Ownership Agreement.** "The co-owners may enter into a limited co-ownership agreement that may run with the land. For example, a co-ownership agreement may provide that a co-owner must offer the co-ownership interest for sale to the other co-owners, the sponsor, or the lessee at fair market value … before exercising any right to partition." Such agreement may also require unanimous approval as set forth in §6.05.

**§6.05 Voting.** "The co-owners must retain the right to approve the hiring of any manager, the sale or other disposition of the Property, any leases of a portion or all of the Property, or the creation or modification of a blanket lien. Any sale, lease, or re-lease of a portion or all of the Property, any negotiation or renegotiation of indebtedness secured by a blanket lien, the hiring of any manager, or the negotiation of any management contract (or any extension or renewal of such contract) must be by unanimous approval of the co-owners. For all other actions on behalf of the co-ownership, the co-owners may agree to be bound by the vote of those holding more than 50 percent of the undivided interests in the Property."

**§6.06 Restrictions on Alienation.** "In general, each co-owner must have the rights to transfer, partition, and encumber the co-owner's undivided interest in the Property without the agreement or approval of any person." Limited exceptions: restrictions required by a lender consistent with customary commercial lending practices are not prohibited.

**§6.07 Sharing Proceeds and Liabilities upon Sale of Property.** "If the Property is sold, any debt secured by a blanket lien must be satisfied and the remaining sales proceeds must be distributed to the co-owners."

**§6.08 Proportionate Sharing of Profits and Losses.** "Each co-owner must share in all revenues generated by the Property and all costs associated with the Property in proportion to the co-owner's undivided interest in the Property."

**§6.09 Proportionate Sharing of Debt.** "The co-owners must share in any indebtedness secured by a blanket lien in proportion to their undivided interests."

**§6.10 Options.** "A co-owner may issue an option to purchase the co-owner's undivided interest (call option), provided that the exercise price for the call option reflects the fair market value of the Property … A co-owner may not acquire an option to sell the co-owner's undivided interest (put option) to the sponsor, the lessee, another co-owner, the lender, or any person related to the sponsor, the lessee, another co-owner, or the lender."

**§6.11 No Business Activities.** "The co-owners' activities must be limited to those customarily performed in connection with the maintenance and repair of rental real property…."

**§6.12 Management and Brokerage Agreements.** "The co-owners may enter into management or brokerage agreements, which must be renewable no less frequently than annually, with an agent, who may be the sponsor or a co-owner (or any person related to the sponsor or a co-owner), but who may not be a lessee. … The determination of any fees paid by the co-ownership to the manager must not depend in whole or in part on the income or profits derived by any person from the Property and may not exceed the fair market value of the manager's services."

**§6.13 Leasing Agreements.** "All leasing arrangements must be bona fide leases for federal tax purposes. Rents paid by a lessee must reflect the fair market value for the use of the Property. The determination of the amount of the rent must not depend, in whole or in part, on the income or profits derived by any person from the Property leased (other than an amount based on a fixed percentage or percentages of receipts or sales)."

**§6.14 Loan Agreements.** "The lender on any debt that encumbers the Property or on any debt incurred to acquire an undivided interest in the Property may not be a related person to any co-owner, the sponsor, the manager, or any lessee of the Property."

**§6.15 Payments to Sponsor.** "Except as otherwise provided in this revenue procedure, the amount of any payment to the sponsor for the acquisition of the co-ownership interest (and the amount of any fees paid to the sponsor for services) must reflect the fair market value of the acquired co-ownership interest (or the services rendered) and may not depend, in whole or in part, on the income or profits derived by any person from the Property."

## 5. What a TIC Agreement MUST Include / MUST NOT Include

| MUST include | MUST NOT include |
|---|---|
| Each cotenant's exact undivided percentage, recited and added to 100% | Treatment of cotenants as "members," "partners," "shareholders" |
| Statement that title is held as TIC under local law (§6.01) | Common business name or "doing business as" |
| Cap on total cotenants (≤35 to fit safe harbor) | Filing Form 1065 or other entity tax return |
| Unanimous-consent list: sale, refi/blanket-lien negotiation, leasing, hiring manager, management contract (§6.05) | Long-term irrevocable management contracts; manager fees tied to net profit |
| Right of each cotenant to transfer, partition, and encumber her own undivided interest (§6.06) | Drag-along, mandatory tag-along, blanket lockup, or buy-sell at non-FMV |
| Pro-rata sharing of all revenues and expenses (§6.08) | Special allocations, preferred return, promote/carry, waterfall above pro-rata |
| Pro-rata sharing of any blanket-lien debt (§6.09) | Disproportionate guaranties of recourse debt |
| ROFR/ROFO at FMV only, with appraisal mechanic | Right of first offer at fixed or formula price below FMV |
| FMV management fee, annually renewable, not contingent on net (§6.12) | Manager that is also the lessee; manager with global power of attorney |
| FMV market rent under bona fide lease (§6.13) | Rent based on net profit, equity appreciation, or net cash flow |
| Lender unrelated to any cotenant, sponsor, manager, or lessee (§6.14) | Sponsor financing or seller financing from a related party |
| Sponsor/acquisition fees at FMV, not contingent on profits (§6.15) | Sponsor backend promote out of net proceeds beyond an arm's-length brokerage fee |
| Each cotenant separately reports income/loss on Schedule E | Common bookkeeping that pools and re-allocates income |

## 6. TIC + JV LLC Side-by-Side on the Same Property

### 6.1 Deed structure

Single deed conveying the fee to:
- "Janet Ng, an unmarried woman, as to an undivided X%;
- Ryan [Last Name], an unmarried man, as to an undivided Y%; and
- [JV Name], LLC, a [State] limited liability company, as to an undivided Z%,
all as tenants in common, X% + Y% + Z% = 100%."

The JV LLC is itself a single cotenant for §6.02 counting purposes.

### 6.2 Co-ownership (TIC) Agreement
A separate, recorded TIC Agreement among Janet, Ryan, and the JV LLC, drafted strictly to the §6.04–§6.15 conditions. Importantly, the JV LLC's internal operating agreement (which governs the active members and includes the promote, preferred returns, manager authority, etc.) lives **inside the JV LLC** and does **not** appear in the TIC Agreement.

### 6.3 Same-economic-outcome problem (matching the JV equity table)

This is the structural tension. The user's stated requirement is that the TIC partners achieve the same economic outcome as the JV partners under the equity table — no more, no less. **A TIC inside the safe harbor cannot accept a partnership-style waterfall.** The legitimate solutions are:

1. **Equate via percentage.** Set Janet's and Ryan's undivided percentages so that pro-rata cash flow equals what they would have received from the JV waterfall at expected returns.
2. **Master-Lease structure.** The JV LLC (or an affiliate) takes a master lease of 100% of the property from all three cotenants. Rent under the master lease is a fixed FMV figure plus a percentage of gross receipts — both permissible under §6.13. The JV LLC, as master tenant, then operates the property and absorbs all promote/waterfall economics inside the LLC. Janet and Ryan receive only their pro-rata share of the FMV master-lease rent.
3. **Operating-Company / Eligible-Passive-Company split** (relevant given the $575K SBA loan).

### 6.4 Financing

Two options:
- **Single blanket-lien loan with all three cotenants as co-borrowers.** Each cotenant signs the note, jointly and severally liable, with proportionate sharing of the debt as required by §6.09. This is what the SBA will require.
- **Pro-rata separate notes.** Less common; rarely accepted by SBA or institutional lenders.

### 6.5 Tax reporting
- Janet and Ryan: Schedule E reporting their pro-rata shares of gross rents, depreciation, interest, and operating expenses. **No K-1 from anyone.** No 1065.
- JV LLC: Files Form 1065 for its members.

### 6.6 1031 inbound and outbound
Janet's and Ryan's qualified-intermediary funds purchase undivided fractional fee interests directly. Because each holds a direct interest in real property, the inbound purchase satisfies §1031(a)(1).

## 7. Federal Banking and SBA Treatment

Under SBA SOP 50 10 and related guidance, when real estate collateral is held by multiple borrowers:

- **Single loan, multiple co-borrowers.** SBA permits a single 7(a) loan to multiple borrowers, "provided each business is a co-obligor."
- **Eligible Passive Company / Operating Company (EPC/OC) structure.** Where the borrower owns real estate but does not operate the business, SBA's EPC/OC framework allows the EPC to be the named real-estate borrower while the OC is co-obligor or guarantor.
- **Single first-priority lien on entire fee.** SBA standard collateral practice requires a perfected first lien on the entire real-property fee.
- **Note structure.** Industry practice (SBA and conventional) is a single note, not pro-rata notes. Pro-rata sharing of the indebtedness for §6.09 purposes is achieved through the TIC Agreement.

## 8. Securities Risk: When Does a TIC Become an Investment Contract?

The federal-securities question is governed by the four-element Howey test (SEC v. W.J. Howey Co., 328 U.S. 293 (1946)):
1. Investment of money,
2. In a common enterprise,
3. With a reasonable expectation of profit,
4. Derived from the entrepreneurial or managerial efforts of others.

**Active, two-party TIC of operating real estate is rarely a security.** Where Janet and Ryan are personally responsible for major decisions and personally vote, they are not relying on the "efforts of others."

**Indicators that push a TIC into "security" territory:**
- Sponsor markets to a pool of unrelated investors;
- Mandatory long-term master lease and management agreement signed at closing;
- Sponsor or affiliate is the master tenant/manager;
- Profit promises in offering materials;
- No real voice for individual cotenants in operations;
- Securitized debt arranged before investors are identified.

**Indicators that keep a TIC out of "security" territory:**
- Two specific, identified cotenants negotiating directly with the JV LLC;
- Each cotenant has §6.05 unanimity rights and §6.06 transfer/partition rights;
- Management contract is annually terminable at FMV (§6.12);
- Master lease, if used, is at FMV with no profit-sharing component (§6.13);
- No offering, no promotion to outside investors, no general solicitation.
