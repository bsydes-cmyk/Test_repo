# RESEARCH MEMO — IRC §1031 Like-Kind Exchange Protocols, TIC Structuring & Coexistence with an Operating JV LLC

**Re:** Real-estate acquisition ($1.9M total / $650K member equity / $575K SBA loan); proposed Tenants-in-Common (TIC) participation by Janet Ng and Ryan using §1031 proceeds, alongside a JV LLC of active participants
**Date:** May 8, 2026
**Prepared from:** IRS, Treasury, and federal court sources

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (5 bullets)

- **§1031 today is real-property only.** TCJA §13303 (Pub. L. 115-97) limited §1031 nonrecognition to real property held for productive use in a trade or business or for investment, effective for exchanges completed after December 31, 2017. Final regulations T.D. 9935 (Nov. 23, 2020) define "real property" via state-law and federal facts-and-circumstances tests.
- **The TIC partners (Janet Ng, Ryan) cannot be members of any partnership** — including the JV LLC — that holds the same property; if the IRS recharacterizes the TIC as a partnership interest, §1031(a)(2)(D) bars exchange treatment ("an interest in a partnership" is excluded property).
- **Rev. Proc. 2002-22 is the controlling administrative safe-harbor.** It is technically only a *ruling-request* procedure, but practitioners and IRS examiners use its 15 conditions in §6 as the de facto compliance checklist for whether a TIC is a co-ownership or a partnership.
- **The JV-LLC + TIC structure can work** if (i) the TIC owners hold direct fee co-tenancy, (ii) economic terms among TICs and the JV are at arm's length, (iii) any management contract between the JV (or sponsor) and the TICs satisfies §6.12 (annual renewal, FMV fees not based on profits, net revenues distributed to owners within three months), and (iv) lease arrangements (e.g., a master lease from the TIC pool to the JV operating entity) satisfy §6.13.
- **Highest-risk items in this deal:** (1) the ~$97K acquisition fee paid to the sponsor must satisfy §6.15 (FMV; not contingent on profits); (2) the $575K SBA loan must be a pro rata obligation of each TIC under §6.04/§6.10 (no entity-level borrower; lender unrelated under §6.14); and (3) the JV LLC, if it is the lessee, must not be a "related party" to the lender, sponsor, or any co-owner.

## 1. REV. PROC. 2002-22 — THE TIC SAFE HARBOR (15 CONDITIONS)

Rev. Proc. 2002-22, 2002-1 C.B. 733 (March 19, 2002) supersedes Rev. Proc. 2002-3 §5.06 and addresses when "an undivided fractional interest in rental real property … is not an interest in a business entity, within the meaning of §301.7701-2(a) of the Procedure and Administration Regulations." Section 6 sets out the 15 conditions; §5 lists information required in a ruling request; §4 defines key terms.

### Section 4 — Definitions (key terms)
- **Co-owner** — "any person that owns an interest in the Property as a tenant in common."
- **Sponsor** — "any person who divides a single interest in the Property into multiple co-ownership interests for the purpose of offering those interests for sale."
- **Blanket lien** — "any mortgage or trust deed that is recorded against the Property as a whole."
- **Property** — the real estate (including any improvements) owned by the co-owners.

### Section 5 — Background (partnership vs. co-ownership distinction)
Rev. Proc. 2002-22 §5 quotes Reg. §301.7701-1(a)(2): "A joint venture or other contractual arrangement may create a separate entity for federal tax purposes if the participants carry on a trade, business, financial operation, or venture and divide the profits therefrom, but the mere co-ownership of property that is maintained, kept in repair, and rented or leased does not constitute a separate entity for federal tax purposes." Section 5 also recites the *Luna v. Commissioner*, 42 T.C. 1067, 1077-78 (1964), eight-factor test for partnership existence.

### Section 6 — The 15 Conditions

| # | Section | Title | Requirement (verbatim or close paraphrase) | Plain-English meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6.01 | **Tenancy in Common Ownership** | "Each of the co-owners must hold title to the Property (either directly or through a disregarded entity) as a tenant in common under local law." Title to the Property as a whole "may not be held by an entity recognized under local law." | Each TIC must be on the deed in undivided fractional shares. A SMLLC for each TIC is OK; a multi-member LLC is not. |
| 2 | 6.02 | **Number of Co-Owners** | "The number of co-owners must be limited to no more than 35 persons." Spouses are treated as one; heirs of a deceased co-owner are one person. | Hard cap. The deal must include only Janet Ng + Ryan + any other deeded TICs (≤ 35 total). |
| 3 | 6.03 | **No Treatment of Co-Ownership as an Entity** | "The co-ownership may not file a partnership or corporate tax return, conduct business under a common name, execute an agreement identifying any or all of the co-owners as partners, shareholders, or members of a business entity, or otherwise hold itself out as a partnership or other form of business entity (nor may the co-owners hold themselves out as partners…)." | No 1065. No EIN for the TIC group. No "partnership" labels in any document. |
| 4 | 6.04 | **Co-Ownership Agreement** | The co-owners may enter into a "limited co-ownership agreement that may run with the land," providing inter alia a right of first offer at FMV before partition and a vote requirement (see §6.05) for major decisions. | A short TIC Agreement is permitted — but it must look like a co-tenancy agreement, not an operating agreement. |
| 5 | 6.05 | **Voting** | "[A]ny sale, lease, or re-lease of a portion or all of the Property, any negotiation or renegotiation of indebtedness secured by a blanket lien, the hiring of any manager, or the negotiation of any management contract (or any extension or renewal of such contract) must be by unanimous approval of the co-owners." All other actions may be by ≥ 50% vote. | Major actions = unanimous; ordinary actions = majority. A unanimous-veto right on sale, lease, debt, and manager hiring is the hallmark of co-ownership. |
| 6 | 6.06 | **Restrictions on Alienation** | "[E]ach co-owner must have the rights to transfer, partition, and encumber the co-owner's undivided interest in the Property without the agreement or approval of any person." Lender-imposed restrictions consistent with customary commercial lending are permitted. | Each TIC must be free to sell or partition. The TIC agreement may include a right of first offer at FMV but cannot truly block transfer. |
| 7 | 6.07 | **Sharing Proceeds and Liabilities upon Sale** | "If the Property is sold, any debt secured by a blanket lien must be satisfied and the remaining sales proceeds … distributed to the co-owners" pro rata. | At sale, the SBA loan gets paid off; each TIC takes its proportionate slice of net proceeds. |
| 8 | 6.08 | **Proportionate Sharing of Profits and Losses** | "Each co-owner must share in all revenues generated by the Property and all costs associated with the Property in proportion to the co-owner's undivided interest in the Property." | No special allocations, preferred returns, waterfall splits, or carried interests for or among the TICs. Each receives strictly its undivided share. |
| 9 | 6.09 | **Proportionate Sharing of Debt** | "The co-owners must share in any indebtedness secured by a blanket lien in proportion to their undivided interests." | Each TIC bears its pro rata share of the $575K SBA debt. The TIC group cannot be a single co-borrower with allocations different from undivided interests. |
| 10 | 6.10 | **Options** | A call option exercisable at FMV is permitted. Put options to a sponsor, lessee, co-owner, or lender are NOT permitted. | No "put" allowing a TIC to force a sale to the JV/sponsor. |
| 11 | 6.11 | **No Business Activities** | "The co-owners' activities must be limited to those customarily performed in connection with the maintenance and repair of rental real property" (referencing Rev. Rul. 75-374). | The TIC group cannot operate a business; it can only own and rent the real estate. Concierge/operating services must be provided by the lessee or independent third party — not by the TIC pool. |
| 12 | 6.12 | **Management and Brokerage Agreements** | The co-owners may enter into a management or brokerage agreement, which "must be renewable no less frequently than annually." Manager may be a sponsor or a co-owner but **not** a lessee. The agreement must (i) require disbursement of all net revenues to the co-owners within 3 months of receipt; (ii) provide for FMV management fees that "may not depend, in whole or in part, on the income or profits derived by any person from the Property." | Management contract must be 1-year term, FMV flat or fixed-percentage-of-gross fee, with sweeps to TICs every quarter. Profit-share comp = recharacterization risk. |
| 13 | 6.13 | **Leasing Agreements** | "All leases must be bona fide leases for federal tax purposes. Rents paid by a lessee must reflect the fair market value for the use of the Property. The determination of the amount of the rent must not depend, in whole or in part, on the income or profits derived by any person from the Property leased (other than an amount based on a fixed percentage or percentages of receipts or sales)." | A master lease from the TIC pool to the JV LLC is permitted only if rent is FMV and not based on net profit/cash flow. Fixed % of gross sales is OK; % of net is not. |
| 14 | 6.14 | **Loan Agreements** | "The lender with respect to any debt that encumbers the Property or with respect to any debt incurred to acquire an undivided interest in the Property may not be a related person to any co-owner, the sponsor, the manager, or any lessee of the Property." | The SBA-approved lender cannot be related (within §267(b)/§707(b)) to any TIC, the sponsor, or the JV LLC lessee. Critical for this deal. |
| 15 | 6.15 | **Payments to Sponsor** | "[T]he amount of any payment to the sponsor for the acquisition of the co-ownership interest (and the amount of any fees paid to the sponsor for services) must reflect the fair market value of the acquired co-ownership interest (or the services rendered) and may not depend, in whole or in part, on the income or profits derived by any person from the Property." | The ~$97K acquisition fee must be defensible as FMV. It cannot be tied to property cash flow or appreciation. Get a contemporaneous fairness opinion if possible. |

> **Drafting caution.** Rev. Proc. 2002-22 §3.02 expressly states that "this revenue procedure provides guidelines for requesting advance rulings" and that satisfaction of the 15 conditions "is not necessarily determinative." Failure to satisfy any condition does not by itself disqualify TIC treatment, but it lifts the safe harbor. In practice, any material deviation should be supported by a tax opinion.

## 2. IRC §1031 — STATUTORY FRAMEWORK (POST-TCJA)

### §1031(a)(1) — General rule
"No gain or loss shall be recognized on the exchange of real property held for productive use in a trade or business or for investment if such real property is exchanged solely for real property of like kind which is to be held either for productive use in a trade or business or for investment."

### §1031(a)(2) — Excluded property
Section 1031 does **not** apply to exchanges of real property held primarily for sale. Importantly, prior law's exclusion of partnership interests is preserved by the structure of the rule and by Reg. §1.1031(a)-1(a)(1): an interest in a partnership is not real property. Pre-TCJA, §1031(a)(2) listed stock in trade, stocks/securities, partnership interests, certificates of trust, and choses in action; post-TCJA, these are excluded by virtue of not being real property.

### §1031(a)(3) — Identification and exchange windows
"[A]ny property received by the taxpayer shall be treated as property which is not like-kind property if —
(A) such property is not identified as property to be received in the exchange on or before the day which is **45 days** after the date on which the taxpayer transfers the property relinquished in the exchange, or
(B) such property is received after the earlier of —
 (i) the day which is **180 days** after the date on which the taxpayer transfers the property relinquished in the exchange, or
 (ii) the due date (determined with regard to extension) for the transferor's return of the tax imposed by this chapter for the taxable year in which the transfer of the relinquished property occurs."

### §1031(b) — Boot (gain recognized)
If the taxpayer receives "other property or money" in addition to like-kind property, gain is recognized to the extent of the FMV of such other property and money.

### §1031(c) — Loss not recognized
No loss is recognized on the exchange even if boot is received; loss must await a fully taxable disposition.

### §1031(d) — Basis
The basis of the replacement property = basis of relinquished property − money received + gain recognized − loss recognized + (any boot given). Liabilities assumed by the other party are treated as money received under §1031(d) flush language.

### §1031(f) — Related-party exchanges
If the taxpayer exchanges with a related person (defined by §267(b) and §707(b)(1)), §1031 nonrecognition is reversed if **either** party disposes of the property within **2 years** after the exchange. There is an exception under §1031(f)(2)(C) for transactions not having tax avoidance as one of their principal purposes.

### TCJA §13303
Section 13303 of Pub. L. No. 115-97 (Dec. 22, 2017) struck "property" and inserted "real property" throughout §1031, effective for exchanges completed after December 31, 2017 (with a transition rule for property transferred before January 1, 2018).

## 3. TREASURY REGULATIONS — §1.1031 SERIES

### Reg. §1.1031(a)-1 — General property qualifications
"As used in section 1031(a), the words 'like kind' have reference to the nature or character of the property and not to its grade or quality." Real property is generally like-kind to other real property regardless of improved/unimproved status.

### Reg. §1.1031(a)-3 (added by T.D. 9935, Nov. 23, 2020) — Definition of real property post-TCJA
Property is real property for §1031 purposes if (i) classified as real property under state or local law where located, (ii) specifically listed in the regulation (e.g., land, buildings, inherently permanent structures, structural components, certain natural products), or (iii) qualifies under the regulatory facts-and-circumstances test. The final rule also allows incidental personal property up to **15% of the FMV** of the replacement real property without disqualifying the exchange.

### Reg. §1.1031(k)-1 — Deferred exchanges and qualified intermediaries (QIs)
Reg. §1.1031(k)-1(b) — Identification rules: written, signed, hand-delivered/mailed/faxed/emailed before midnight day 45.
- **Three-property rule** §1.1031(k)-1(c)(4)(i)(A): identify up to 3 properties of any value.
- **200-percent rule** §1.1031(k)-1(c)(4)(i)(B): identify any number with aggregate FMV ≤ 200% of relinquished property FMV.
- **95-percent rule** §1.1031(k)-1(c)(4)(ii): identification is honored even if more than 3 properties (and >200%) are listed if the taxpayer actually receives ≥ 95% of identified properties' FMV.

Reg. §1.1031(k)-1(g) — Four safe harbors against constructive receipt: (g)(2) security/guarantee arrangements; (g)(3) qualified escrow accounts and qualified trusts; (g)(4) **qualified intermediaries**; (g)(5) interest and growth factors.

§1.1031(k)-1(g)(6) — The **(g)(6) restrictions**: the QI agreement must expressly limit the taxpayer's right to receive, pledge, borrow, or otherwise obtain the cash held by the QI before the earlier of 180 days or the receipt of all identified replacement property.

§1.1031(k)-1(k) — Disqualified persons include the taxpayer's agent (e.g., attorney, accountant, real-estate agent within 2 years of the transfer) and persons related under §267(b)/§707(b).

### Reg. §1.1031(j)-1 — Multiple-property exchanges
The regulation requires properties to be sorted into "exchange groups" of like kind/like class. Section 1031 rules are then applied separately to each exchange group.

## 5. FORM 8824 — REPORTING

The exchanger files Form 8824 with the income-tax return for the year the relinquished property is transferred. Per the 2025 Instructions:

- **Part I** — describes the like-kind property given up and received, and key dates.
- **Part II** — related-party transactions (§1031(f)).
- **Part III** — realized gain, recognized gain, and basis of replacement property:
  - **Line 15:** Cash received, FMV of other property received, plus net liabilities assumed by the other party = boot received.
  - **Line 19:** Realized gain or loss.
  - **Line 22:** Recognized gain = lesser of realized gain or boot (Line 15) (under §1031(b)).
  - **Line 24:** Deferred gain.
  - **Line 25:** Basis of like-kind property received = adjusted basis of relinquished property + boot paid + recognized gain − boot received.

Each TIC files **its own** Form 8824 reflecting only its undivided fractional interest. The JV LLC does not file an 8824 for the TIC owners' interests.

## 6. PARTNERSHIP / TIC DISTINCTION — REGS §301.7701-1, §301.7701-3, AND THE RULINGS

### Reg. §301.7701-1(a)(2)
"A joint venture or other contractual arrangement may create a separate entity for federal tax purposes if the participants carry on a trade, business, financial operation, or venture and divide the profits therefrom, but the mere co-ownership of property that is maintained, kept in repair, and rented or leased does not constitute a separate entity for federal tax purposes."

### Reg. §301.7701-3 — Check-the-box
A domestic eligible business entity (e.g., a multi-member LLC) is, by default, classified as a partnership unless it elects to be a corporation.

### Rev. Rul. 75-374, 1975-2 C.B. 261
Co-ownership of an apartment building between an insurance company (X) and a REIT (Y), each holding a 50% undivided interest, with a third-party manager (Z) providing customary services from the gross rents, **was not a partnership**. Customary services (maintenance, repair, customary tenant services) do not convert co-ownership into partnership.

### Rev. Rul. 79-77, 1979-1 C.B. 448
Three individual co-owners of a commercial building subject to a long-term **net lease** to a single tenant were held not to constitute a partnership. Net lease + no active services = co-ownership.

### Other guidance
- *Luna v. Commissioner*, 42 T.C. 1067 (1964) — eight-factor partnership test.
- PLR 200327003 (relying on Rev. Rul. 75-374; treated TIC interest as eligible §1031 replacement).
- *Allison v. Commissioner*, T.C. Memo. 1988-376 — co-ownership of land found *not* to be a partnership.

## 7. RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN TIC OWNERS AND THE OPERATING LLC — WHAT IS PERMISSIBLE

There are three documented frameworks consistent with Rev. Proc. 2002-22:

**A. Master Lease structure (preferred for this deal).** The full TIC pool — the JV LLC (as one TIC) plus Janet Ng plus Ryan plus any other deeded TICs — leases the entire property to a separate operating LLC under a single bona fide master lease. Under §6.13, the master lease must be at FMV rent that is not based on net income or profits (a fixed percentage of gross sales is OK; net cash flow is not). The lessee may not be the manager (§6.12). Per Rev. Rul. 79-77, a net master lease cleanly insulates the TIC pool from being deemed to "carry on a trade or business."

**B. Management Agreement structure.** The TIC pool engages a third-party (or sponsor-affiliated) manager to operate the property under a §6.12-compliant management agreement: (i) **renewable no less frequently than annually**; (ii) FMV fees *not* based on income/profits; (iii) **net revenues distributed to co-owners within 3 months** of receipt; (iv) the manager cannot also be a lessee.

**C. TIC Agreement.** A short, recorded TIC Agreement is permissible under §6.04 to: govern voting (§6.05), establish a right of first offer at FMV before partition (§6.06), and provide for sharing of proceeds at sale (§6.07).

**Crucial structural rule for the JV.** The JV LLC must NOT also be the lessee under the master lease AND the manager. Under §6.12 the manager cannot be a lessee.

## 8. DEBT / FINANCING CONSTRAINTS

The $575K SBA loan creates several Rev. Proc. 2002-22 issues:

1. **§6.09 Pro-rata responsibility.** Each TIC must be liable on the loan in proportion to its undivided interest.
2. **§6.05 Unanimity for blanket-lien negotiation.** TIC group must unanimously approve refinancings.
3. **§6.06 Encumbrance right preserved.** Each TIC must have the right to encumber its undivided interest separately, subject to lender restrictions "consistent with customary commercial lending practices."
4. **§6.14 Lender independence.** Lender cannot be related (within §267(b)/§707(b)) to any co-owner, sponsor, manager, or lessee.
5. **Note structure.** Although Rev. Proc. 2002-22 does not literally prohibit a single note co-signed by all TICs, the better practice is *separate* notes (or a single note explicitly allocating obligations pro rata).
6. **Boot from debt assumption.** If a TIC's incoming SBA-loan share is *less* than the debt on its relinquished property, the difference is boot under §1031(b) and Reg. §1.1031(d)-2.
7. **SBA-specific concern.** SBA 7(a) loans to a multi-borrower TIC require Form 1919/1920 disclosures from each TIC owner, and SBA personal guarantees from 20%+ owners.

## 9. REPORTING AND PITFALLS — WHAT KILLS §1031 TREATMENT

### Common audit triggers

1. **"Drop-and-Swap" close to closing.** Distributing TIC interests out of a partnership shortly before sale, then having distributee partners exchange. *Chase v. Commissioner*, T.C. Memo. 2004-142 (denied §1031 where partnership distributed property to partner at closing); compare *Magneson v. Commissioner*, 753 F.2d 1490 (9th Cir. 1985) (allowing §1031 + immediate contribution to partnership).
2. **Constructive receipt of proceeds.** *Crandall v. Commissioner*, T.C. Summ. Op. 2011-14 — denied §1031 where seller used a title company to "hold" funds rather than a QI.
3. **Related-party exchanges.** *Teruya Brothers, Ltd. v. Commissioner*, 124 T.C. 45 (2005), aff'd 580 F.3d 1038 (9th Cir. 2009); *Ocmulgee Fields, Inc. v. Commissioner*, 132 T.C. 105 (2009), aff'd 613 F.3d 1360 (11th Cir. 2010).
4. **Identification failures.** *Dobrich v. Commissioner*, T.C. Memo. 1997-477 — backdated identifications denied.
5. **Holding-period intent.** *Adams v. Commissioner*, T.C. Memo. 2013-7; *Reesink v. Commissioner*, T.C. Memo. 2012-118.
6. **TIC recharacterized as partnership.** If TIC fails Rev. Proc. 2002-22 §6 conditions and IRS finds a partnership in fact, §1031(a)(2) bars the exchange.

### Red Flags That Break §1031 (checklist)

- Multi-member LLC holds title for the TIC pool.
- Any partnership tax return (Form 1065) filed for the TIC group.
- TIC group EIN holding common bank account beyond §6.12 management-agreement scope (3-month sweep mandatory).
- Profit/loss allocations not strictly proportionate to undivided interests.
- Special allocations, preferred returns, waterfalls, or carried interests among the TICs.
- Debt allocations not pro rata; single co-borrower note without pro-rata allocations.
- Lender related to any co-owner, sponsor, manager, or lessee.
- Sponsor fee tied to property income or profits.
- Acquisition fee not supportable as FMV.
- Management contract longer than one year, or with profit-share comp.
- Manager and lessee are the same entity (e.g., JV LLC serves both roles).
- Lessee paying rent based on net income or net cash flow.
- Put options to sponsor, lessee, lender, or co-owners.
- TIC agreement waives partition rights or transfer rights beyond customary lender restrictions.
- Constructive receipt of cash by the exchanger (no QI; QI is "disqualified person").
- Identification missed or 180-day deadline missed.
- More than 35 co-owners.

## 10. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR JANET NG AND RYAN AS TICs ALONGSIDE THE JV LLC

**Capital stack (working assumption):** Total cost $1.9M = $650K member equity (which presumably funds the JV LLC's TIC share, including the ~$97K acquisition fee paid to sponsor) + $575K SBA debt + Janet/Ryan TIC equity (the 1031 proceeds making up the balance).

**Title.** Each TIC must be on the deed. Recommended vesting:
- "[JV LLC name], a [State] limited liability company, as to an undivided X% interest, and Janet Ng, a single woman/married woman as her separate property, as to an undivided Y% interest, and Ryan [Last Name] [marital status], as to an undivided Z% interest, all as tenants in common."
- A single-member LLC for each individual TIC is permissible (Rev. Rul. 2004-77; Rev. Proc. 2002-22 §6.01 allows ownership "through a disregarded entity").

**TIC count.** The JV LLC counts as 1 co-owner under §6.02 even if it has many members behind it. Janet + Ryan + JV LLC = 3 co-owners.

**Key documents:**
1. **TIC Agreement** between the JV LLC, Janet, and Ryan, recorded against title. Drafted to mirror Rev. Proc. 2002-22 §6.04-6.07.
2. **Master Lease** from the TIC pool (lessor) to a separate operating LLC (lessee), on FMV terms.
3. **Management Agreement** (only if there is no master lease) between the TIC pool and a third-party or sponsor-affiliate manager, satisfying §6.12.
4. **Loan documents** structured so that each TIC is severally liable for its pro-rata share.
5. **Sponsor/Acquisition Fee Documentation** establishing FMV of the ~$97K fee.

**Crucial structural rule for the JV.** The JV LLC must NOT also be the lessee under the master lease AND the manager. Under §6.12 the manager cannot be a lessee.

**Economic differentiation for Janet and Ryan.** Their return profile is fundamentally different from the JV's active partners: they receive (i) pro-rata rental income net of pro-rata SBA debt service and pro-rata maintenance/insurance/tax, and (ii) pro-rata share of net sale proceeds at exit. They do not share in the JV LLC's management profits, leasing-up alpha, or developer carry.

**Tax filings.** The TIC pool files no return. Each TIC reports its proportionate income and expense on its own Schedule E. Janet and Ryan each file Form 8824 reflecting only their fractional interests.

**Holding period after exchange.** No statutory minimum, but to defend "held for investment" intent, the TIC interest should be held for 24+ months before any subsequent disposition.

**Bottom line.** The structure is workable but tightly constrained. The TIC partners must accept that in exchange for §1031 deferral, they give up the JV's active-deal upside.
