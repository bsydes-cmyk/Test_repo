# Real, Documented Precedents Matching the User's Deal Structure

The user's deal — a $1-3M acquisition by a 6-8 member active JV LLC, with a TIC sidecar for 1031 investors, ~25-35% SBA financing, three-bucket equity, multi-role management team in hospitality/STR — sits at the intersection of four bodies of authority: (i) Williamson/Howey securities case law, (ii) IRS guidance under Rev. Proc. 2002-22 and PLRs, (iii) SBA EPC/OC structuring rules, and (iv) public TIC operating agreements filed with the SEC.

---

## Section 1 — TIC + Operating-LLC Structures (Real Agreements / Court Summaries)

### 1A. Bluerock Stonehenge 23Hundred (Berry Hill, Nashville) — TIC + Joint Venture LLC
- **Citation/Source:** Bluerock Residential Growth REIT, Inc., Form 10-K, Tenancy in Common Agreement filed as Exhibit 10.191 (Dec. 9, 2014); Redemption Agreement dated Dec. 9, 2014.
- **Direct URLs:**
  - TIC Agreement: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1442626/000114420415013992/v402637_ex10-191.htm
  - TIC Agreement (separate exhibit): https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1442626/000114420415020620/v406246_ex10-3.htm
  - Redemption Agreement (Justia): https://contracts.justia.com/companies/bluerock-residential-growth-reit-inc-3548/contract/418992/
- **Facts:** Bluerock and Stonehenge formed BR Stonehenge 23Hundred JV, LLC (a Delaware JV LLC) to develop a 266-unit multifamily project in Berry Hill, Tennessee. In December 2014, a redemption transaction converted Bluerock Growth Fund's LLC interest into a direct TIC interest in the underlying real estate, leaving the remaining members in the JV LLC and the Bluerock Growth Fund vehicle as a TIC alongside them.
- **Structure:** JV LLC + redeemed Bluerock Growth Fund as TIC co-owner, with a Property Management Agreement and a Development Agreement (Stonehenge as developer/manager) assumed by the redeeming party.
- **Outcome:** Property sold for $61.2M in January 2015; deal generated approximately a 48.1% net IRR. No regulatory challenge.
- **Lesson for the user:** This is a real, publicly filed example of an LLC JV co-existing alongside a TIC sidecar at the same property, with development-management and property-management contracts running between the JV and a manager. The exhibit is essentially the form a 1031-investor TIC sidecar takes when bolted onto an LLC JV.

### 1B. PLR 200513010 — TIC + Manager + Blanket Mortgage with up to 35 Co-Owners
- **Citation/Source:** IRS Private Letter Ruling 200513010 (April 1, 2005).
- **Discussion:** https://www.robertsandholland.com/news-and-insights/finally-irs-issues-ruling-under-rev-proc-2002-22-involving-multi-tenant-building-with-blanket-mortgage/
- **Facts:** A multi-tenant net-leased commercial building was owned by 35 co-owners as TICs, subject to a blanket mortgage, and managed by a sponsor-affiliated manager.
- **Structure:** TICs (35 owners) + sponsor-affiliated manager + blanket mortgage. The Manager had discretion to lease without the consent of the co-owners.
- **Outcome:** IRS held: (i) multi-tenant occupancy did not create a partnership; (ii) blanket mortgage was acceptable; (iii) Manager's discretion to lease without co-owner consent did not disqualify TIC status; (iv) sponsor's short-term TIC ownership was permissible.
- **Lesson:** Leading PLR for a TIC + management LLC fact pattern in which the manager has real operational discretion.

### 1C. PLR 200327003 — TIC + Co-tenancy Agreement with Manager-at-FMV Fee
- **Citation/Source:** IRS PLR 200327003 (released July 3, 2003).
- **Direct URL:** https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-wd/0327003.pdf
- **Facts:** A company acquired a fee interest in commercial real property and entered into a Co-tenancy Agreement among the co-owners with a separate Management Agreement.
- **Structure:** TIC + manager (co-owner-affiliated, paid fair-market fees), with unanimity required for sale, lease, refinance of any blanket lien, and appointment of any manager — and majority approval (>50%) for everything else.
- **Outcome:** IRS ruled the co-ownership was not a business entity and the TIC interests qualified as 1031 replacement property.
- **Lesson:** The user can borrow this exact decision-rights split (unanimity for "Five Big Things," majority for everything else) for the TIC sidecar.

### 1D. Rev. Rul. 75-374 — Older Authority on the Customary-Services Line
- **Direct URL:** https://www.taxnotes.com/research/federal/irs-guidance/revenue-rulings/rev-rul-75-374/dbfd
- **Facts/holding:** Two co-owners (insurance company + REIT) of an apartment project hired an unrelated corporate manager. Customary services do not create a partnership, but additional services (i.e., hospitality-style services like daily housekeeping, room service, concierge) DO.
- **Lesson:** This is the single most important caution for a hospitality/STR/boutique-hotel TIC sidecar. The user almost certainly cannot put the operating side of a hotel inside the TIC; instead, the TIC must own only the real estate and lease it (typically to the JV LLC as an EPC-OC operator) so the "additional services" are furnished by the OC, not by the TIC owners.

## Section 2 — Federal Court Cases on JV LLC / Partnership = Security under Williamson/Howey

### 2A. Williamson v. Tucker, 645 F.2d 404 (5th Cir. 1981) — The Foundational Case
- **Direct URL:** https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/us-5th-circuit/2179492.html
- **Facts:** Godwin Investments syndicated undivided interests in undeveloped Texas real estate to a small number of buyers. The interests were transferred into joint ventures.
- **Structure:** Several joint ventures, each with a few "venturers" who held undivided interests; Godwin was the manager/promoter.
- **Outcome:** Fifth Circuit announced the now-standard Williamson three-prong test.
- **Lesson:** This is the case the user's structure must beat. To stay outside the securities laws, the user's 6-8 active members need real, exercisable powers, genuine business experience, and the right to exit.

### 2B. SEC v. Shields, 744 F.3d 633 (10th Cir. 2014) — Williamson Applied to Modern JVs
- **Discussion:** https://www.corporatesecuritieslawblog.com/2014/03/tenth-circuit-looks-past-general-partnership-labels-in-agreements-to-determine-whether-certain-investments-constitute-securities/
- **Facts:** Shields and his company GeoDynamics syndicated oil-and-gas joint venture interests with offering documents that explicitly told investors they were general partners. Investors were given the right to remove the managing venturer, to terminate the partnership, and to inspect records — but not to bind the venture, spend funds, or interpret contracts.
- **Outcome:** Tenth Circuit held the SEC adequately pleaded that the JV interests WERE securities despite the GP labels.
- **Lesson:** Labels do not save a deal. Each of the user's seven role-holders needs a real, documented seat, real removal rights, and a fallback plan if the managing member is removed.

### 2C. Robinson v. Glynn, 349 F.3d 166 (4th Cir. 2003) — LLC Member NOT a Security
- **Direct URL:** https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/349/166/636867/
- **Facts:** Robinson invested $25M in GeoPhone Co., LLC. He negotiated for two of the three board seats (he held one himself), and served as treasurer.
- **Outcome:** Fourth Circuit affirmed dismissal. Because Robinson had bargained for and exercised significant managerial control — antithetical to passivity — his LLC interest was NOT a security.
- **Lesson:** This is the case the user wants to look like.

### 2D. SEC v. Merchant Capital, LLC, 483 F.3d 747 (11th Cir. 2007)
- **Direct URL:** https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/483/747/573857/
- **Facts:** 28 RLLPs; 485 partners total; partners were inexperienced public investors.
- **Lesson:** Confirms Williamson prong 2.

### 2E. Holden v. Hagopian, 978 F.2d 1115 (9th Cir. 1992) and Koch v. Hankins, 928 F.2d 1471 (9th Cir. 1991)
- **Direct URLs:** https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/978/1115/183616/ ; https://openjurist.org/928/f2d/1471/koch-v-j-hankins
- **Lesson:** Ninth Circuit adopted Williamson; Holden was NOT a security (real powers + majority votes), Koch (jojoba farm partnerships) WAS a security.

## Section 4 — SBA-Financed Deal with TIC / EPC-OC Layering

### 4A. SBA SOP 50 10 (most recent: SOP 50 10 8, eff. June 1, 2025) — Recognition of TIC as a Single EPC
- **Direct URL:** https://www.partneresi.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/SOP-50-10-8-effective-6.1.2025-Final.pdf
- **Authoritative text confirms:** A tenancy in common in which multiple individuals or entities own real estate as TICs is treated as one EPC. The OC must immediately occupy at least 60% of the property, the EPC must lease 100% to the OC, and the rent cannot exceed loan payments + taxes + insurance + reasonable maintenance.

### 4B. Best-Practice Counsel Treatment (Starfield & Smith)
- https://starfieldsmith.com/2019/01/best-practices-a-refresher-on-epc-oc-transactions/
- https://colemanreport.com/understanding-sbas-epcoc-rules/

## Section 5 — Hospitality/STR/Boutique-Hotel JV with 5-10 Active Partners

### 5A. Hocking v. Dubois, 885 F.2d 1449 (9th Cir. 1989) (en banc) — Condo Hotel + Rental Pool
- **Direct URL:** https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/885/1449/144091/
- **Facts:** Buyer of a Hawaii condo unit signed up for a rental pool agreement with Hotel Corporation of the Pacific.
- **Outcome:** En banc Ninth Circuit held that when a condominium is sold with an option to participate in a rental pool, the package can be an investment contract.
- **Lesson:** This is THE warning case for hospitality/STR structures. If the user's TIC sidecar investors are pitched on the deal because of the JV LLC's hotel-management expertise, the offering can flip into a security irrespective of the TIC label.

### 5B. CityCenter Boutique Hotel Holdings, LLC — Public Operating Agreement
- **Direct URL:** https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1372786/000119312511260109/d233905dex38.htm
- **Lesson:** Publicly filed boutique-hotel JV LLC operating agreement; the user can study its waterfall, manager-removal, and fee provisions.

### 5C. Hospitality Investors Trust — REIT Bankruptcy
- **Direct URLs:** https://dm.epiq11.com/case/hospitalityinvestorstrust/info
- **Lesson:** Bankruptcy docket is a goldmine of publicly available hotel SPV operating agreements.

**Honest caveat:** Could not find a published federal opinion squarely involving a $500K-$1M hospitality/STR JV with 5-10 small active partners that matches the user's facts.

## Section 6 — Acquisition Fee Precedent

### 6A. SEC CF Disclosure Guidance Topic No. 6 — Non-Traded REIT Acquisition Fees
- **Direct URL:** https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/disclosure-guidance/cf-disclosure-guidance-topic-no-6
- **Holding:** SEC staff requires non-traded REITs to disclose actual sponsor compensation paid (including acquisition fees) on a tabular basis.

### 6B. SEC v. Merchant Capital — Sponsor Compensation
- The Eleventh Circuit's emphasis on sponsor-controlled fees as evidence that the venturers lacked real power applies directly to acquisition-fee structuring.

### 6C. SEC v. Sky Group USA LLC, No. 1:21-cv-23443 (S.D. Fla.)
- **Direct URLs:** https://www.sec.gov/files/litigation/complaints/2022/comp25443-boulos.pdf and https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/litigation-releases/lr-25234
- **Facts:** Sponsor raised $66M in unregistered promissory notes; misappropriated funds.
- **Lesson:** SEC's modern focus on sponsor-fee disclosure in real-estate-adjacent unregistered offerings.

## Section 7 — Closest Single Match

The closest single match overall is the **Bluerock Stonehenge 23Hundred** structure. It is the only public, real-document precedent that bolts a TIC onto a multi-member JV LLC at the same property, with separate development and management agreements.

The doctrinal closest match is the combined **Robinson v. Glynn (4th Cir.) + Holden v. Hagopian (9th Cir.) + Williamson (5th Cir.)** trio.

## Closest Matches Ranked

1. **Bluerock Stonehenge 23Hundred TIC + JV Structure** — Public TIC + JV LLC at one property; directly usable form.
2. **PLR 200513010** — TIC + sponsor-affiliated manager with real discretion + blanket mortgage; the IRS-blessed model.
3. **Williamson v. Tucker, 645 F.2d 404 (5th Cir. 1981)** — The framework the user's structure must satisfy.
4. **Robinson v. Glynn, 349 F.3d 166 (4th Cir. 2003)** — The "this is what survives" precedent.
5. **PLR 200327003** — The decision-rights split (unanimity for the Big 5; majority for everything else).

**Honorable mentions:** SEC v. Shields for what NOT to do; Hocking v. Dubois for the hospitality/rental-pool warning; Rev. Rul. 75-374 for the customary-vs-additional-services line; SOP 50 10 8 for the SBA EPC/OC + TIC blueprint.
